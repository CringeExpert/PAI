Local-Cohen-Macaulay DG-rings with unequal finitistic projective dimensions

Abstract.

For every pair of integers d,n≥1d,n\geq 1, we construct a commutative noetherian local-Cohen-Macaulay DG-ring AA such that dimH0​(A)=d\dim H^{0}(A)=d, amp⁡(A)=n\amp(A)=n, fpd⁡(A)=d−n\fpd(A)=d-n, and FPD⁡(A)=d\FPD(A)=d. This gives a negative answer to the implication from local-Cohen-Macaulayness to equality of the two finitistic projective dimensions in a question of Bird, Shaul, Sridhar, and Williamson.

Strong Lefschetz property of the Kirchhoff algebra of the complete tripartite graph K1,1,nK_{1,1,n}

Abstract.

By Whitney’s 2-isomorphism theorem, the cycle matroids of two graphs are isomorphic if and only if one graph can be transformed into the other by finitely many applications of two operations: separation or identification at a cut-vertex, and twisting. The complete tripartite graph whose vertex set is the disjoint union of two singletons and an nn-element set is invariant, up to isomorphism, under these operations. In this paper we prove that the Macaulay inverse system (the Kirchhoff algebra) determined by the basis generating polynomial of the cycle matroid of such a graph has the strong Lefschetz property over a field of characteristic 00. The proof reduces the non-degeneracy of the higher Hessians to the ranks of disjointness matrices of families of subsets (the Gottlieb–Wilson theorem) and to the representation theory of the symmetric group in relation to the Johnson scheme.

Maximal common divisors in monoid algebras

Abstract.

A commutative cancellative monoid has the MCD property if every nonempty finite subset has a maximal common divisor, and it has the MCD-finite property if every such subset has only finitely many maximal common divisors up to associates. We study these properties in monoid algebras. We first identify atomic and antimatter families of rank-11 torsion-free MCD monoids. We then prove that, for a field FF and a torsion-free monoid MM, the MCD property ascends from MM to F⁡[M]F[M] whenever the group algebra F⁡[𝒢⁡(M)]F[\mathscr{G}(M)] satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal ideals. We also record the pre-Schreier case as a consequence of classical GCD ascent. In contrast, for every prime pp, we construct a reduced rank-33 MCD monoid MM such that 𝔽p​[M]\mathbb{F}_{p}[M] is not an MCD domain, and an atomic rank-11 MCD monoid whose algebra over 𝔽p\mathbb{F}_{p} is not atomic. We further prove that the prime reciprocal monoids considered here are MCD-finite and that MCD-finiteness ascends to polynomial extensions. Finally, for every prime pp, we construct a rank-11 torsion-free q-GCD monoid whose algebra over 𝔽p\mathbb{F}_{p} is not q-GCD, where the q-GCD property requires at most one maximal common divisor up to associates for every nonempty finite subset.

On Stanley-Reisner Rings with Minimal Betti Numbers

Abstract.

We study simplicial complexes with a given number of vertices whose Stanley-Reisner ring has the minimal sum of Betti numbers. We prove that every full subcomplex of them is homotopy equivalent either to a point or to a sphere. Moreover, we prove that such kind of simplicial complexes coincide with those having minimal Taylor resolutions.

Leafrians, Poisson structures, and Frobenius splittings on moduli spaces

Abstract.

The Poisson TT-Pfaffians of Bott–Samelson varieties, G/UG/U, and G/BG/B produce Frobenius splittings, but only by a local, coordinate-bound argument. We isolate the local mechanism: a weighted trace pairing on the deformation space of a fat point, whose top coefficient factors over the blocks. At the members of a presentation, polyvector fields on the base XX transfer to the moduli space MM. Applied to a Poisson bivector, this produces a Poisson bivector, extending Bottacin’s structure from surfaces to arbitrary dimension. Applied to a top degree polyvector ss and wedged with a transverse Euler frame, it produces an anticanonical section on MM, which we call the leafrian. When XX is a Poisson surface, we prove that the leafrian splits the moduli of stable sheaves, of principal GG-bundles, and of instantons if and only if ss Frobenius-splits the base. The mechanism itself is dimension-free. The construction also recovers and generalizes the splittings of the Lie-theoretic examples: the rr-matrix need not satisfy the classical Yang–Baxter equation.

Optimal cosmological cross-correlation analysis
with U-Net-based processing

Abstract

Context. Cosmological cross-correlation analyses are commonly based on two-point statistics, which are optimal for Gaussian random fields but do not capture all relevant information in the presence of non-Gaussianity, which is ubiquitous in late-time cosmic fields. This motivates investigations of higher order statistics, and one method is to use machine learning to construct a correlation-optimized tracer field that can utilize cross-correlation information beyond two-point statistics.

Aims. Using a tracer field as input and a paired target field as part of the cost function, we test whether a U-Net can learn a transformed tracer field that maximally correlates with the target field.

Methods. We train a 1D U-Net on paired simulated fields and study controlled cases based on Gaussian and log-normal random fields. We train and quantify performance using the Pearson correlation coefficient with the target field and compare the network output with the analytic linear solution known in the Gaussian limit.

Results. For Gaussian fields, the U-Net reproduces the expected linear solution closely and behaves as an effective matched filter in frequency space. For log-normal fields, the network departs from the linear estimator and achieves an improved correlation with the target field, increasing the mean Pearson correlation from 0.8570.857 for the linear estimator to 0.9530.953 for the U-Net output. This indicates that the network is sensitive to information beyond the power spectrum.

Conclusions. These controlled 1D experiments show that U-Net-based tracer transformation can effectively extract correlation-relevant information beyond the power spectrum, providing a promising framework for enhancing cosmological cross-correlation analyses.

A cosmological intercept tension in local distance ladders

Abstract

The Hubble tension motivates direct tests of the internal consistency of the local distance ladder. We perform forward and backward consistency checks between Type Ia supernovae in Cepheid host galaxies and the Hubble-flow supernova sample using Pantheon+ data. Within flat Λ\LambdaCDM, we impose Planck-CMB priors on one sample, infer the supernova absolute-magnitude calibration, and propagate it to the other sample to recover the Hubble constant, which should match the input value if the second and third rungs of the distance ladder align perfectly. We examine the impact of host-galaxy peculiar velocities using redshift corrections based on the Manticore-Local reconstruction. These corrections shift the recovered H0H_{0} from 74.30−1.20+1.1874.30^{+1.18}_{-1.20} to 71.37−1.18+1.2371.37^{+1.23}_{-1.18} km​s−1​Mpc−1\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}} in the forward analysis and from 60.83−0.97+0.9860.83^{+0.98}_{-0.97} to 63.25−0.93+0.9563.25^{+0.95}_{-0.93} km​s−1​Mpc−1\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}} in the backward analysis, bringing both results closer to the input CMB constraint. Comparing the cosmological intercepts of the magnitude-redshift relation inferred from two rungs of the distance ladder before and after peculiar-velocity corrections, we find that the significance of the discrepancy decreases from 6.8​σ6.8\sigma to 3.9​σ3.9\sigma in the forward check and from 7.3​σ7.3\sigma to 4.3​σ4.3\sigma in the backward check. Peculiar-velocity corrections therefore partially alleviate the intercept tension, while a residual discrepancy persists within the adopted framework. Our results highlight the importance of local velocity modeling and motivate further tests of sample selection and calibration systematics in assessing distance-ladder consistency.
